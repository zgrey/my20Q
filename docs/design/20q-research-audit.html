Why this audit

my20Q is, at its core, a constrained 20-questions player: converge on one specific need through yes / no / kinda / not-sure answers from a person with aphasia. That game has real theory — information theory fixes how fast convergence can go, the Rényi–Ulam line fixes what noise does to it, and a recent LLM literature documents exactly how language models fail at it. This audit lines the current engine (the 5W1H facet controller, v2 + the opposition button, June 2026) up against that theory and answers three questions: what mirrors the literature, what is getting in the way, and what is still missing.

The theory, in four results

① Optimal questioning = entropy halving (Huffman)

For a prior π over candidates, the optimal yes/no question strategy is a Huffman code: expected questions sit between H(π) and H(π)+1 (Twenty (simple) questions, STOC 2017). The greedy version is the familiar rule: ask the question whose yes-probability is closest to ½. Every question with p far from ½ buys less than one bit, and the cost compounds:

Fig 2 — Why split-in-half is the law. Expected questions to isolate 1 of 64 candidates = 6 / H₂(p), if every question you ask has yes-probability p. Asking near-certain questions (p→1) is the mathematical description of the confirmation farming our June-10 trial exhibited (who=Zach pumped to +7.5 on re-confirmations) — each such question is worth a fraction of a bit.

② Noisy answers: never trust a single "no" (Rényi–Ulam)

The classical results assume a truthful oracle. With lies or errors — the Rényi–Ulam "liar" setting, and noisy 20-questions for target localization — hard elimination is catastrophic and the optimal strategies switch to weighted/soft updates plus repetition for verification. In aphasia this is not a corner case: answer reliability is a known clinical limitation of yes/no questioning (Aphasia Library).

Fig 3 — One wrong "no" must not kill the truth. If each answer is wrong with probability ε, hard elimination keeps the true value alive with probability (1−ε)k — at ε=10% the truth is dead 88% of the time by question 20 (solid red). The engine's additive scores with elimination floor −2 behave like a random walk that must drift to −2 before the truth drops out; the ultimate ruin probability is (ε/(1−ε))² (gambler's ruin) — ≈1% at ε=10% (green). The June-7 single-no-elimination experiment sat on the red curve and was rolled back the same day; the floor has been load-bearing ever since.

③ LLMs cannot play this game implicitly

The LLM-era literature converges on one finding: probing LLMs with 20-questions games shows weak multi-turn planning — repeated/reworded questions, premature guessing, and failure to integrate negative answers — and the fix across papers is to externalize the state: track belief outside the model and score candidate questions by expected information gain, or plan over simulated answer branches (Uncertainty of Thoughts). These published failure modes are not abstractions for us — they are literally our trial autopsies: the same question asked 7× (June 9), guess-enumeration down one dead avenue (June 10), and a hallucinated subject accumulating score ("Aaron", June 9).

④ The clinical layer (SCA) bounds everything

Supported Conversation for Adults with Aphasia (Aphasia Institute) endorses exactly this interaction: yes/no questions, one at a time, progressing general → specific, with time to respond and written/visual support — and it warns against relying on yes/no alone, recommending verification of important answers and fixed-choice questions as the step beyond yes/no (Flint Rehab). The theory above must operate inside these constraints: the optimal question is worthless if the person cannot reliably answer it.

2 · What is getting in the way

Six places where the current mechanics fight the theory. None are fatal; most are magnitude problems (counts and absolute thresholds standing in for evidence measures) rather than shape problems.

F1 · Counting confirmations is not weighing evidence conflict

Synthesis needs min_yes_for_synthesis=5 yeses (then 3 new ones per retry). Sequential testing says: stop when the posterior odds cross a threshold, not when a tally fills — five low-information yeses (the farming trial) and two decisive splits are worlds apart, yet the count treats them alike. The informative-yes patch and the board-readiness path are both implicit admissions of this. Direction: let board-readiness (a margin condition — already evidence-shaped) lead, and demote the yes-count to a safety floor.

F2 · Absolute thresholds on a growing scale conflict

facet_ready_points=2.0, split_margin=1.0, floor −2 are fixed while total board mass grows every turn. A +2 lead after 6 answers is strong evidence; after 50 answers it is noise — but the engine reads both as "determined". Bayesian scoring normalizes by construction; ours deliberately doesn't (that simplicity is worth keeping), so the thresholds should at least scale with answered-query count or the slot's total absolute mass. This also touches the deliberate volatile-layer choice: every round starts from a uniform zero prior — correct for privacy and for the "today ≠ last week" rule, but it means the engine cannot encode even structural priors (e.g. "when matters less than how" for people topics) except through the focus policy.

F3 · The question itself is chosen blind to information gain conflict

Code picks the slot; the LLM freely picks which contender and phrasing — precisely the choice EIG methods score and ours doesn't. Gate 4 rejects only the degenerate case (zero gain) after generation, and the exploration coin (explore_decay^(yeses+1), below) injects randomness that is blind to the board: it can force a fresh probe at the exact moment a split would be decisive.

Fig 5 — The exploration schedule. A reasonable bandit-style ε-decay — but it consults only the yes-count, never the board. An EIG-scored candidate set (gap 1) would subsume it: true exploration is just "the highest-gain question happens to probe something new".

F4 · The repeat gate outlaws verification conflict with noise theory

Rényi–Ulam strategies repeat questions on purpose — under noise, re-asking is how you verify — and SCA practice re-checks important answers. Our gate bans every reword for the whole round; the only verification the engine ever does is the synthesis proposal itself, where a wrong load-bearing yes is expensive to unwind (it takes a failed utterance + a pin cycle). The Dice-0.8 overlap can also block legitimate drills that share an anchor: "Will Zach come over today?" after "Will Zach come over?" scores 6/7 ≈ 0.86 → rejected as a repeat, even though it asks a new slot (when). Fix shape: a code-initiated, gate-exempt verify turn ("Just to double-check —") for a pair about to be woven into an utterance, plus letting a question through when it asserts a slot the prior question didn't.

F5 · The rotation guard can break drill ladders conflict

MAX_CATEGORY_RUN=2 cured why-hammering, but taxonomy descent — the engine's own best win (body → leg → foot → toe, June 8) — needs 3+ consecutive same-slot questions. Today that ladder would be interrupted after two rungs and forced lateral; the pin directive yields to the same guard. Fix shape: exempt drill/pin from rotation while the focused slot's leader score is rising — rotate on stall, not on count.

F6 · Either/or is banned; the clinic recommends it deliberate divergence

Gate 1 rejects either/or questions because the answer surface is four buttons — an either/or can't be answered with yes/no. But SCA guidance places fixed-choice questions as the natural step after yes/no, and information theory agrees: a two-option forced choice is a guaranteed split-in-half (Fig 2's optimum, by construction). Worth keeping the ban for now — and revisiting when the patient-facing surface exists: two tappable pictogram cards are an either/or, and the engine could emit choice-kind queries for exactly the split directive.

3 · Remaining gaps

Ranked by expected payoff per unit of engineering.

G1 · True EIG question selection the big one

We pick the slot; the literature picks the question. UoT-style: generate candidate questions, estimate each one's yes-probability against the board, ask the one closest to a half split (or max expected entropy drop). Cheap version that fits our latency budget: the format pass already returns JSON — have it emit 2–3 candidate questions with their asserted slots in the same single call, then code picks the candidate whose asserted pair sits nearest the slot's score median (a proxy for p≈½), preferring candidates that assert an unestablished pair. Zero extra LLM calls; the gates run on the chosen one as today.

G2 · An answer-noise model + per-patient calibration high value

Points (+1 / +0.5 / −1 / 0) encode noise implicitly. A small confusion model — P(button | true state of the asserted pair) — would let the same four buttons update the board as log-likelihood ratios, make the floor and thresholds principled, and (this is the unique part) be calibrated per patient from the recorded dataset: the 3-tier recordings already capture every (question, answer, eventual outcome) triple needed to estimate this person's yes-reliability. That is squarely inside the privacy invariant — the dataset is local, and the calibration is the stable layer informing how evidence is weighed, never what is guessed.

G3 · Verification turns pairs with F4

One deliberate, repeat-gate-exempt re-ask of each load-bearing pair before it is woven into an utterance ("Just to double-check — is it Zach you want to come over?"). Rényi–Ulam says re-ask under noise; SCA says verify what matters; the engine currently has no path to do either except burning a whole synthesis attempt. Budget: at most one verify per pair per round, only for pairs entering a proposal, skipped when the pair was confirmed twice already.

G4 · A coarse→fine ladder inside each slot structural

"a picture" and "the large picture in the hall" currently compete as flat rivals in the same slot — the fine value must out-score the coarse one it refines. A parent link (fine value → the coarse contender it specializes) would let leaders be subtree sums, drills descend the tree explicitly (Huffman-flavored), and the 06-09 near-miss ("who/what surfaced only at q~55") become structurally impossible: confirming the coarse node early is recorded progress, not a rival to beat.

G5 · A noisy-answerer regression bench cheap, do soon

scripts/bench_reasoning.py already replays both trial targets. Add an ε-noise wrapper around the simulated answerer (flip yes↔no with ε ∈ {0, 5%, 10%, 20%}) and report queries-to-converge distributions per engine version. Fig 1 then stops being hand-collected: every engine change gets a curve, and claims like "the direction layer helps" become measured instead of vibed. (Per the gemma4 lesson, the simulator must be a strong model — a weak simulated oracle is itself a noisy oracle with unknown ε.)

G6 · Fatigue-aware stopping clinical

"Never self-end" is the right safety posture, but the literature's cost-sensitive variants suggest a softer middle: once core slots are determined, each further modifier question should have to justify itself (expected gain vs. fatigue cost) — concretely, stop enriching when/where/why and go to synthesis once the expected utterance no longer changes. Aphasia fatigue is real; a 50-question round is a failure even when it converges.

Verdict

The architecture is the one the literature recommends — externalized belief, code-side policy, soft noise-tolerant scoring, hard anti-repetition, explicit confirmation, clinical SCA framing. Nothing structural needs to be torn up. The friction is concentrated in magnitudes (counts and absolute thresholds standing in for evidence measures: F1, F2) and in two missing behaviors — information-gain question choice (G1) and verification under noise (F4/G3). The direction layer and ⇄ Opposite are theory-consistent and novel; they are also untrialed, and the kitchen-target re-run is the experiment that judges them.

Watch in the next trial

  • Does the sign-flip nudge surface them-for-me within ~10 questions on the kitchen target (vs. never in 52)?
  • Does the caregiver reach for ⇄ Opposite unprompted, and does the flipped question read naturally aloud?
  • Do the F4/F5 frictions bite visibly — a blocked legitimate drill (watch for diagnostics after gate rejections), or a rotation-forced lateral mid-ladder?
  • Round length vs Fig 2's bound: with ~5 live contenders per slot and 2 core slots, a clean run should converge in well under 20 questions. Over 30 means F1–F3 are costing real bits.
